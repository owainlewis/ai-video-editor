## What the reference reels open with

- "You can now run a whole agency for exactly zero dollars." (Agency Agents): a big outcome and a price, with the on-screen title "Run A Whole Agency / Inside Claude Code".
- "If you're using Claude Code without these 5 MCP servers, you're leaving most of its power on the table." (MCP): calls out a loss the viewer is suffering now.
- "Here are the five most powerful Claude connectors." (connectors): a list promise, paid off from the first frame with connectors switching on.
- A real GitHub repo card on screen in frame 1, with "team of AI agents" and "3 security holes" by second 4 (Cloudflare).
- "Google just released Pomelli… automates your marketing, completely free." (Pomelli): big name, new thing, outcome, price.

## The patterns that work

1. **Contrarian command.** "Stop doing X." It works when X is something the viewer really does.
2. **Name the mistake.** "Most people do X. That's the mistake."
3. **Surprising number plus the question it raises.** "Claude writes 90% of its own code. So who reviews it?"
4. **Loss or stake.** What the viewer is losing, risking or paying right now.
5. **Big name, new thing, outcome.** Say in one breath who made it, what it is and what it does for you.
6. **The viewer's own question,** backed by proof that others ask it too.

## Rules of thumb

- The first line lands inside 2 seconds and is about the viewer, not the product.
- The product's name comes after the hook, never as the hook ("It's called AX" is not a reason to stay).
- Frame 1 shows the hook as text, or as the real thing (the repo, the number), not a logo alone.
- Never open with "Today", "In this video", "If you want to…" or "X is essential".
- Every claim in the hook is true and checked (no "just released" for a repo seven months old).
- The body has to pay off the hook within about 10 seconds, or the hook reads as bait.

## Hooks for the Google AX reel (week of 2026-10-26)

The current line, "Google is building a Kubernetes for AI agents", is a decent pattern 5 (big name, new thing) but says nothing about the viewer. Options, best first:

1. **"Stop running AI agents on your laptop."** (contrarian command). Then: "Google built a better place for them. A Kubernetes for AI agents, called AX." It sets up the laptop problem the body already explains.
2. **"Your AI agents are a security risk, and you can't even see what they're doing."** (stake). Then: "Google's answer is AX…"
3. **"Do you know what your AI agents are doing right now? Or what they're costing you?"** (the viewer's question)
4. **"Google built a Kubernetes for AI agents, and it's made to run billions of them."** (pattern 5 plus the README's own number)
5. **"Agents don't belong on your laptop. Google just showed where they do."** (name the mistake; but "just" is not accurate for a repo public since March, so say "Google built where they do")

What the winners share:

1. **A concrete outcome with a price anchor**: "completely free", "Fire your $10,000 marketing team", "takes one minute".
2. **Big name, new thing, free**, often with a contrast: "while everybody's been obsessing over ChatGPT, Google silently released…".
3. **Momentum and proof**: "people are now…", "it's blowing up", "the lead engineer of Claude posted…". The same topic framed as "X can now do Y" did 60x worse; one pair proves little, but the gap is wide.
4. Every one ends on a keyword CTA ("Comment TV"), and comments run 5 to 35 per 1,000 views on those.

What flops: list openers ("Here are five ways…") and catalogues of names.

Owain's own ad-lib on the AX reel, "Google just open-sourced a new tool for running thousands of AI agents in the cloud", is pattern 2 and the strongest hook we have for it. Only "just" is wrong for a repo public since March 2026.
