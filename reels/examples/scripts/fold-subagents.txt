Anthropic keeps seeing the same mistake in company AI agents. Too many subagents.

They compared designs across several big deployments. One agent with skills often beat a team of subagents. Better answers, and often cheaper and faster. One customer saw it run about twice as fast.

Here's how it goes wrong. Every team builds its own subagent, so the agent ends up a copy of the org chart. And every team adds a line to the system prompt. Anthropic calls it the Frankenprompt.

So what do they do instead?

One agent, one context window. It sees short descriptions of its skills and tools, and only loads the full thing when it needs it. That's part of how they cut Claude Code's system prompt by over eighty percent.

But don't delete your subagents yet.

They still win in three cases. When one agent's context gets too full. When work can run in parallel. Or when one agent keeps picking the wrong tools.

My take: start with one agent, and add a subagent only when your evals prove you need it.

Anthropic's advice is the same. Build a small eval, even ten examples. Fold in one subagent. Keep it only if the folded version scores worse.
